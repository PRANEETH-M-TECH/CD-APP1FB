Hello. That is a really interesting question. These water tanks, often called Cheruvulu in Telangana, are very old and clever structures our ancestors built to capture and store rainwater.

It was not a complex, step by step factory process, but rather a simple and effective engineering solution using natural resources.

Based on how tanks were traditionally built, here are the easy steps for construction:

1. Find the Stream. The builders would first identify a suitable location, usually a small natural stream or a path where rainwater naturally flowed, which is called run off water.
2. Gather Materials. They needed strong materials that were locally available, primarily stones and mud.
3. Build the Wall or Bund. They constructed a very strong, thick wall using the stones and mud.
4. Block the Flow. This strong wall was built across the small stream or flow path.
5. Form the Lake. The wall was positioned strategically so that by building a barrier on just one side, the water flowing down the stream would be trapped, backing up and forming a large, useful reservoir or lake behind the wall.

This simple method allowed them to collect water that could be used throughout the year for drinking and irrigating fields, even in dry regions.
