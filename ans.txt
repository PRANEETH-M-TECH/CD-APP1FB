not salts.

the acidic and basic qualities are

crystallization.

Hello there! That is a great question that gets right to the heart of understanding chemical substances. Acids, bases, and salts are three distinct types of substances we encounter frequently.

Here is a clear differentiation between them, based on their properties as discussed in our textbook:

### 1. Acids (Acidic Nature)

Acids are characterized primarily by how they react and what they are made of:

*   **Indicators (Litmus Test):** Substances that are acidic in nature turn **blue litmus paper to red**.
*   **Taste/Sensation:** While we should never taste unknown lab chemicals, many food substances containing acids (like lemon juice or curd) are typically **sour** in taste. The sensation from an ant bite is also due to the presence of formic acid.
*   **Types:** Acids can be **natural acids** (found in plants and animals) or **mineral acids** (like Hydrochloric acid, Sulphuric acid, and Nitric acid).

### 2. Bases (Basic Nature)

Bases are often defined by their physical feel and their contrasting reaction to indicators:

*   **Indicators (Litmus Test):** Substances that are basic in nature turn **red litmus paper to blue**.
*   **Touch/Feel:** Bases are typically **soapy to touch** (like soap water or caustic soda solution).
*   **Examples:** Common bases are found in household items like soap and glass cleaners.

### 3. Salts

Salts are often formed when acids and bases interact, but they possess properties that make them unique:

*   **Formation:** Salts are generally produced when acids and bases are mixed, leading to the process of **neutralization**.
*   **Neutrality is Variable:** A common misconception is that all salts are neutral. However, salts **need not always be neutral**. Based on how they affect indicators, salts can be classified into three categories:
    1.  **Acidic Salts:** Salts which change **blue litmus to red**.
    2.  **Basic Salts:** Salts which change **red litmus to blue**.
    3.  **Neutral Salts:** Salts which affect **neither blue nor red litmus papers**.
*   **Examples:** While sweat is salty because we lose salts through excretion, the textbook also notes that not all neutral substances are salts (e.g., sugar solution is neutral but not a salt).

***

### Summary of Differentiation (Litmus Test)

The most reliable way to differentiate these three categories using indicators (like litmus) is their specific reaction:

| Substance | Effect on Blue Litmus Paper | Effect on Red Litmus Paper |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Acid** | Turns RED | No change (Stays Red) |
| **Base** | No change (Stays Blue) | Turns BLUE |
| **Neutral Salt** | No change (Stays Blue) | No change (Stays Red) |
