That is an excellent question! Understanding the Mean Sea Level is fundamental to measuring any height on Earth.

The **Mean Sea Level (M.S.L.)** is the standard reference height (or 0 meter height) used globally to measure the height of all places on land.

Here is a detailed explanation based on how the concept is derived and used:

### 1. Why is MSL Necessary?

The level of the sea is not fixed; it is constantly changing. This is due to natural phenomena like **high tides and low tides**. Because the water level is either rising or falling, scientists cannot simply pick one moment in time to define the sea level.

### 2. How is MSL Calculated?

In order to establish a stable reference point, the following steps are taken:

*   **Frequent Measurement:** The level of the sea is carefully measured at frequent intervals over a long period.
*   **Averaging:** Scientists observe these high and low levels and calculate the **mean** (or average) of all these measured heights.
*   **Standard Reference:** This single, calculated average level is known as the Mean Sea Level (M.S.L.), and it is taken as the **0 meter height**.

### 3. Importance and Use of MSL

The Mean Sea Level is critically important because it serves as the universal baseline for all land measurements:

*   **Calculating Land Heights:** All heights on land, whether a small hill, a large mountain, or a plateau, are calculated *from* the sea level.
*   **Universal Standard:** Since all the seas and oceans in the world are connected to each other, the sea level (M.S.L.) is taken to be the standard reference point all over the world.

You may have seen this concept applied directly if you visit a railway station. The display board often mentions the height of that location in meters "M.S.L." (e.g., 150 MSL), indicating how high that spot is above the calculated average sea level.
