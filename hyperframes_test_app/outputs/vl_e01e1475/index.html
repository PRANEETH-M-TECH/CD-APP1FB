How are MLAs Elected?

Understanding State Assembly Elections

Hello! You asked about elections. Our textbook focuses on how Members of Legislative Assembly, or MLAs, are elected in State Assembly elections. Let's explore this important process step-by-step!
MLA Candidate Qualifications
Citizen of India
Completed 25 years of age
Should not hold any office of profit under government
Possess other qualifications as prescribed by law
First, let's see who is eligible to stand for elections to become an MLA. There are a few important rules to follow.

Political Parties
Field their own candidates.
Represent a party's ideology and agenda.
Release election manifestos with programmes and promises.
Independent Candidates
Do not belong to any political party.
Contest elections based on their personal merit.
Do not have a party manifesto, usually focus on local issues.
Candidates can belong to a political party or stand on their own. Let's understand the difference between them.
Election Campaigns
Creating Election Manifestos (descriptions of programmes and promises)
Holding public meetings to address voters
Conducting door-to-door visits to meet voters personally
Supporters actively campaign for their chosen candidates
Before voting day, candidates try to convince voters! They share their plans and promises with everyone through various activities.
Casting Your Vote
Voting is kept a secret (Secret Ballot) to ensure free choice.
Voters use Photo Identity Cards for verification.
Votes are cast using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs).
On election day, eligible citizens cast their votes. This is a very important and secret process to ensure fairness and transparency.
