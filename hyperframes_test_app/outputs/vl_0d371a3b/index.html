The Rule of the Nizams

A Glimpse into Hyderabad's History

Hello, young historians! Today, we're embarking on an exciting journey to explore the fascinating rule of the Nizams in Hyderabad. Let's uncover their story!
Asaf Jahi Dynasty (Hyderabad State)
Founder: Chin Qulich Khan (Nizam-Ul-Mulk)
Established In: 1724 CE
Origin: Mughal Subedar of Deccan
After the Mughal Empire weakened, its governors asserted independence. One powerful figure, Chin Qulich Khan, also known as Nizam-Ul-Mulk, founded the Asaf Jahi dynasty in Hyderabad in 1724.

The Nizams constantly fought the Marathas and Mysore rulers to create their independent state. However, these continuous wars made them reliant on the British, eventually leading to a loss of their full independent status.
Colonial Modernisation (Hyderabad)
Influence: British Officials
Period: Late 19th Century
Outcome: Embarkment on Development
British Perception: Loved Hyderabad Culture
Under the influence of British officials, the Nizams adopted 'colonial modernization'. The British were fond of Hyderabad's unique culture, and this period saw significant development in the late 19th century.
Hyderabad's Reformers
Salar Jung I (PM): • Prime Minister (1853-1883) • Imbibed colonial development schemes
Mir Mahabub Ali Khan: • Sixth Nizam • Period marked with many reforms • Founded Asafia Library, Victoria Memorial Orphanage, Mahabubia Girls School
Salar Jung, a prominent Prime Minister from 1853 to 1883, championed colonial development schemes. The Sixth Nizam, Mir Mahabub Ali Khan, also marked his period with numerous reforms, including building significant institutions.
VII Nizam's Rule
Popular Demand: Liberal administrative reforms
Nizam's Stance: Determined to protect feudal power structure
Towards the later period, the Seventh Nizam, despite popular movements for liberal reforms, was determined to protect the existing feudal power structure.
