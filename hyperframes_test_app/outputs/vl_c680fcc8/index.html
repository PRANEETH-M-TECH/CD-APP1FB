Paper Mills: Where Paper Begins
Primary Raw Material: Wood (Bamboo, Soft Wood, Subabul)
Historical Location: Near Forests (rich in bamboo)
Current Sourcing: From Distant Places & Farmed Trees (like Subabul)
Essential Resource: Large Quantity of Clean Water
The most important raw material for making paper is wood! Originally, paper mills were built close to forests where trees like bamboo and other softwoods were abundant. But with changing times, mills now source wood from further away, even encouraging farmers to grow specific trees like Subabul.
Step 1: Chipping the Wood
Action: Large pieces of wood are cut into small chips.
Tools Used: Large, powerful machines (can chip a lorry load in ~30 mins).
Output: Uniform wood chips, separated by size.
The very first magical step is called 'Chipping'. Imagine huge logs arriving at the mill! Giant machines then cut these big pieces of wood into tiny, uniform 'chips'. It's so efficient, a whole lorry load of wood can be chipped in about 30 minutes!
Step 2: Making Wood Pulp
Location: Fibre Line Section
Process: Wood chips are boiled with chemicals in large vessels.
Result: Chips turn into a soft, thin pulp of fibres (like cotton).
Finishing: The liquid pulp is then whitened.
After chipping, the small wood chips move to the 'Fibre Line' section. Here, they're boiled with special chemicals in massive vessels. This process transforms the hard wood chips into a soft, thin, liquid 'pulp' – much like cotton fibres! Finally, this liquid pulp is whitened, ready for the next stages.
