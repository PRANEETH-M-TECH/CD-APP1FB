The President's Path to Power

Understanding Presidential Elections in India

Hello, future leaders! You've asked a super important question: How is the President of India elected? While our textbook talks about monsoons, rainfall, and general elections for MLAs and MLCs, it mentions that the Governor is appointed by the President. This tells us the President is a very important figure! Let's explore how they are chosen.
President of India
Head of State: Acts as the first citizen of India.
Guardian of Constitution: Ensures the country functions by its rules.
Supreme Commander: Leads the Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force).
Elected By: The Electoral College (a special group of elected representatives).
The President is the head of our country and the supreme commander of the armed forces, acting as the guardian of our Constitution. Unlike general elections where all citizens vote directly, our President is elected by a special group of people. This group is called the 'Electoral College'.

Who Elects the President? The Electoral College!

So, who are these special people in the Electoral College? It includes only the 'elected' members from both houses of our Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) and the 'elected' members from the Legislative Assemblies of all states and Union Territories. Nominated members do not vote!
Presidential Voting System
Proportional Representation: Ensures votes from different states carry different weights based on population.
Single Transferable Vote: Voters rank candidates in order of preference instead of just picking one.
Secret Ballot: Like other elections, voting is confidential.
Goal: To elect a President who truly represents the nation.
The voting method is quite unique! Instead of a simple 'one person, one vote' system, they use 'Proportional Representation with Single Transferable Vote'. This means the value of each vote can differ, ensuring that states with larger populations have their votes weighted appropriately, leading to a fair outcome for the entire nation.
