Perimeter vs. Area Challenge

Comparing Fields with Equal Perimeters

Welcome to a geometric challenge! Today, we'll explore the fascinating relationship between perimeter and area, comparing a square field and a rectangular field. Our goal is to determine which field encloses a larger area, despite sharing the same perimeter.
Imagine two fields: a square one, with each side measuring 60 meters. And a rectangular field, with a known length of 80 meters, but its width is still a mystery. Both fields, we're told, have the exact same perimeter.

First, let's calculate the perimeter of the square field. A square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is simply four times the length of one side. With a side of 60 meters, the square's perimeter is 240 meters.

Now, we know the rectangular field has the same perimeter: 240 meters. The formula for a rectangle's perimeter is 2 times (length + width). We have the length, so we can solve for the unknown width.

With all dimensions known, let's calculate the area. The area of a square is found by multiplying the side length by itself. For our 60-meter square, this gives us 3600 square meters.

Next, we calculate the area of the rectangular field. We now know its length is 80 meters and its width is 40 meters. Multiplying these two dimensions gives us an area of 3200 square meters.

Comparing Areas

Now for the comparison! The square field has an area of 3600 square meters, while the rectangular field has an area of 3200 square meters. Clearly, the square field has a larger area.
